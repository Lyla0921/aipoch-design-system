# Cover System

## Shared Canvas Rules

- Work at 900×383 pixels.
- Reserve about 7% of the canvas on every side as a quiet edge zone.
- Place the title in a stable high-contrast area; use two lines at most unless the user explicitly asks otherwise.
- Keep optional metadata compact and visually separate from the title.
- Prefer one focal image, one graphic system, or one product crop. Do not combine several unrelated visual metaphors.

## Product Launch

**Default direction:** product/interface.

Use a product screenshot, device crop, feature close-up, or a restrained abstract technical graphic. Give the title approximately half the canvas and make the product evidence visibly specific. Use a black, white, or surface field with a precise yellow highlight; use orange only for a small status or release cue.

Choose one of these layout families:

1. **Split proof:** left-aligned title block; right-side product crop with soft containment.
2. **Editorial interface:** large product crop in the background; solid readable title panel or clean overlay.
3. **Feature signal:** large title; one small product detail plus a thin diagrammatic line or highlight.

## Event

**Default direction:** editorial image.

Show the event's actual atmosphere, speaker, location, or a strong contextual image if supplied. If no event image exists, use a typographic poster composition with date and location as compact metadata. Keep logistics clear without letting it outrank the title.

Choose one of these layout families:

1. **Date strip:** title over a calm image field; date and location in a compact mono strip.
2. **Poster field:** large headline with one yellow block or underline; small event metadata aligned to an edge.
3. **Image window:** full-bleed contextual crop with a protected title zone and small identity mark.

## Viewpoint Or Content Article

**Default direction:** typographic or abstract graphic.

Translate the idea into a single visual metaphor, structured diagram fragment, editorial crop, or a type-led composition. Avoid literal stock illustrations of “thinking,” “AI,” or “business growth.” Make the article’s claim or question readable first.

Choose one of these layout families:

1. **Type-led statement:** oversized title, ample white or surface space, one restrained yellow emphasis.
2. **Concept crop:** title on one side; a cropped conceptual photo or abstract graphic on the other.
3. **Structured argument:** headline with one diagram-like form, line system, or concentric pattern used as evidence rather than decoration.

## Selection Guidance

Use type-led or poster layouts for short, declarative titles. Use split or image-window layouts when a supplied asset is important. Use structured layouts when the topic is analytical. Never reuse the exact same layout family for consecutive covers unless the user requests a campaign series.
